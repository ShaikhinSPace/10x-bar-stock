Count what

A re-skin of the 10X stock app as a piece of industrial equipment: hard rules, exposed grid, monospaced figures, one loud colour. Every screen is a control surface — the number that needs attention is the biggest thing on it, and nothing else competes. Deep royal blue does all the talking; the rest is ink, paper and structure.

Why a ledger, not a feed

The point of the app is catching leakage between what leaves the storeroom and what each bar actually has. That only works if every move is recoverable, so the log never collapses or hides an entry — it colours it.

Each type gets one glyph and one hue: ↗ give, ↓ receive, ⇄ transfer, ⚠ waste, ✎ count. Deliveries fold their many lines into one batch tag.

The redesign in one paragraph

This treats stock control as instrumentation, not an app store product. The grid is exposed rather than softened, figures are set in a monospace so columns of counts line up honestly, and colour is rationed — deep royal blue for the primary surface and every "act now" control, muted ochre and slate to tell the two bars apart, and a single brick red reserved for out-of-stock and wastage. Partials like 0.25 and 1.87 are treated as first-class figures because that is how the bars are really counted. Nothing is decorative: every rule, wash and pill encodes a real piece of state.

Visual study only — no application logic, data or functionality is implemented here. Screens mirror the live app's five tabs: Dashboard, Stock, Delivery, Activity, Manage.
